RADIOBIOCOATS

Multi-technical fabrication of radiopaque bioactive glass coatings for biomedical applications

Implant coatings that bond to bone, resist bacteria, and show up on X-ray images.

FunGlass · Alexander Dubček University of Trenčín, Slovakia · 2024–2026 · Principal Investigator: Dr. Germán A. Clavijo-Mejía

Watch the video Read the publications
Funded by the European Union – NextGenerationEU

Why it matters

Metallic implants are often coated with bioactive materials that help bone grow onto them. These coatings leave three needs open, and RADIOBIOCOATS addresses them in one material.

See the implant

Most bioactive coatings have an X-ray contrast similar to bone, which makes it difficult to follow the position of an implant and its condition after surgery. Radiopaque coatings improve that follow-up.

Bond to bone

Bioactive glasses form a layer of apatite, the mineral of bone, in contact with body fluids, and they support healing at the interface between implant and tissue.

Resist bacteria

Implant-associated infection is a leading cause of revision surgery. The antibacterial function is built into the coating itself.

What we did

The material

Bioactive glasses that contain bismuth oxide, an element that absorbs X-rays strongly. We studied two families: silicate glasses of the 45S5 type and borate glasses of the 13-93B3 type.

Three coating routes

Atmospheric plasma spray, electrophoretic deposition (including composites with PEEK), and spray coating, all on titanium alloy, the metal used in many orthopedic implants.

Rigorous methods

Statistical design of experiments for the processing windows, a radiopacity protocol based on ISO 13116, and in vitro tests with bone-like cells and with Escherichia coli and Staphylococcus aureus.

Results

All results below are published or protected by a granted patent.

4.2×

Higher radiopacity

Silicate 45S5-type glasses with up to 12.2 wt. % Bi₂O₃ stay amorphous, show up to 4.2 times higher radiopacity, and still form apatite in simulated body fluid.

Ceramics International 50 (2024)
0.22 → 3.06 mm Al

Borate glasses

In 13-93B3 borate glass, adding 20 wt. % Bi₂O₃ raised radiopacity from 0.22 to 3.06 mm of aluminium equivalent, and an apatite-like layer still formed after 7 days.

Ceramics International (2026)
Cells + bacteria

Safe for bone-like cells, hostile to bacteria

Bone-like cells kept similar viability across compositions, a content of 6 wt. % Bi₂O₃ induced secretion of the growth factor VEGF, and antibacterial activity increased with bismuth.

Ceramics International 51 (2025)
31–53 %

A processing window for plasma spray

One parameter, the stand-off distance, explains 31 to 53 % of the variation in microhardness, porosity, and thickness of plasma-sprayed bismuth-glass coatings, which defines the processing window.

Results in Surfaces and Interfaces 24 (2026)
PEEK + glass

Composite coatings

Composite coatings of PEEK and bismuth-containing borate glass were deposited electrophoretically on titanium and presented at FunGlass Schools and doctoral seminars.

FunGlass School 2/2025; Doctoral Seminar 2026
